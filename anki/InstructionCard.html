2swap’s Connect 4

If you have somehow made it here without watching my latest Connect 4 YouTube video, reading the explanation document, and viewing the solution graph, please do! They teach the technical foundation underlying this deck.

Deck Structure

This deck is built from the solution graph and contains two parts:

  • Learn: cards teach a unique suggested move for every non-leaf node in the graph according to the weak solution.
  • Practice: randomly shuffled opening play. Play as Red (player 1) against diverse opponents with a high coverage of the graph.

Openings Included

The Learn cards are further subdivided by opening name. Included openings correlate well with those seen in actual play, such as the 6-1 opening, candlesticks openings, and D3–D4 openings.

Some other common openings (for example, the 5-4 and the Swedish) are omitted because the solution graph follows alternative lines that avoid those openings, thereby saving the need to learn them.

Since no one has ever diagrammed the space of Connect 4 openings, names in common English have not really standardized, and instead modern players most often simply list the columns played in order to communicate an opening (i.e. "4366".)

Since the graph very cleanly shows openings in discernible branches and clusters, I took the creative liberty to name them based on either the shape of the disks on the board, or their "anatomical" location on the graph (e.g. Fist, Palm, Crown, etc.) I like to think of the graph as a kind of octopus with a head (the center-column openings,) tentacles (426 and 415 openings,) and two fists (4366 openings.) Do you see the resemblance?

Openings are loosely ordered according to my opinion of their popularity, also keeping similar openings close together. You can jump around in the deck without much extra trouble.

To view the openings in the solution graph, press O inside the solution graph to see the openings color-coded on the graph. The Learn cards are organized by these same branches.

It's not that hard!

The whole solution graph contains ~8700 nodes. However, we don't need to learn any nodes which are Yellow-to-play, which are ~85% of the graph!

Furthermore, there may be ~1300 Learn cards, but most are duplicated up to mirror symmetry to aid recognition. It’s not 1300 unique openings.

This deck intentionally omits explicit Steady State solutions for leaf nodes. Instead of memorizing those endgames, I recommend you "learn by playing." With practice, a human player can develop a sufficient intuition for endgames which renders rote memorization unnecessary.

Furthermore, keep your eyes out for trends and repeating patterns- these will make memorization much easier!

For new Anki users

Anki is (ironically) known for its challenging learning curve. The point of Anki is to facilitate memorizing lots of information, and it is a great tool for that job. However, Anki's high customizability makes it quite easy for the user to shoot themselves in the foot by attempting to memorize data that is poorly formatted and thereby challenging to remember, or not follow a consistent schedule.

To learn this deck, you need to do all of your reviews every day. Reviews are represented by the green numbers on the Anki home screen. If you skip a day, you will have a large backlog of reviews to catch up on. If you are overwhelmed by the number of reviews on default Anki settings, click the gear next to the "2swap's Connect 4" deck on the home screen, select "Options," and reduce the "New cards/day" setting. You should expect approximately 4 times as many reviews per day as your new cards/day setting.
